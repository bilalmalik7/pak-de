Is education really free in Germany for Pakistani students?

Yes. Most public universities in Germany charge no tuition fees, including for Pakistani and other non-EU students. You pay a semester contribution of roughly €100–€400, which often includes a public transport ticket. Exceptions: Baden-Württemberg charges non-EU students €1,500 per semester, and TU Munich charges international tuition fees. We'll always tell you the exact cost of every programme on your list.

Can I study in Germany with a low CGPA?

Often, yes. Many universities of applied sciences and programmes without strict cutoffs review your whole profile: projects, work experience, SOP and recommendation letters. Private universities, Studienkolleg and Ausbildung are further routes. Use our free Profile Analyzer to see your German grade and the routes that fit.

Can I study in Germany without IELTS?

Some German universities accept a Medium of Instruction (MOI) letter from your Pakistani university, or other tests such as PTE, TOEFL or Duolingo for selected programmes. However, an IELTS Academic score of 6.5 opens far more doors and supports your visa file, so we usually recommend it.

How much is the blocked account for Germany?

For a German student visa you must show about €11,904 per year (€992 per month) in a blocked account (Sperrkonto). The official amount is revised periodically, so we always confirm the latest figure with you before you transfer funds.

When are the intakes in Germany?

There are two intakes: Winter semester (starts September/October; applications typically close around 15 July, some international programmes earlier) and Summer semester (starts March/April; applications typically close around 15 January). We plan your timeline backwards from your target intake.

Can I study a bachelor's in Germany after FSc or A-Levels?

After FSc/ICS, students usually go through a one-year Studienkolleg or complete one year of a related BS degree in Pakistan for direct entry. With suitable A-Levels you can often apply directly. We map the right route for your subjects and marks.

Can I work while studying in Germany?

Yes. International students can work 140 full days or 280 half days per year, roughly 20 hours a week during the semester. After graduation you can apply for an 18-month residence permit to look for a job.

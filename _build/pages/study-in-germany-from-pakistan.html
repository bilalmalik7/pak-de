💶 Is education in Germany free for Pakistani students?

For most programmes at public universities, yes. Germany abolished general tuition fees at public universities, and that applies to Pakistani and other non-EU students too. You pay a semester contribution of roughly €100–€400, which usually covers student services and often a regional public transport ticket.

There are exceptions. The state of Baden-Württemberg (Heidelberg, KIT, Stuttgart, Freiburg, Mannheim) charges non-EU students €1,500 per semester, TU Munich charges international tuition, and private universities charge fees. We show the real cost of every programme on your shortlist.

📋 Requirements for Pakistani students

For a Master's in Germany

  • A 16-year bachelor's degree (BS, BE, BBA, MBBS, or BA/BSc + MA/MSc) in a related field
  • A competitive German grade for top universities (convert yours with our CGPA calculator)
  • IELTS Academic 6.5 (or accepted alternative), or German B2–C1 for German-taught programmes
  • SOP / motivation letter, Europass CV, and usually 2 recommendation letters
  • HEC-attested degree and transcripts; uni-assist / VPD where required

For a Bachelor's in Germany

  • After FSc / ICS / ICom: Studienkolleg (foundation year) or one year of a related BS in Pakistan
  • After A-Levels: direct admission is often possible with the right subject combination
  • IBCC-attested certificates and language proof (German B1 for most Studienkollegs)

🛂 Germany student visa process from Pakistan

  1. Get admission (or conditional admission / Studienkolleg acceptance).
  2. Open your blocked account and arrange health insurance.
  3. Prepare your file: attested documents, language certificates, CV and motivation statement.
  4. Apply for the national (D) student visa through the German Embassy Islamabad or the Consulate General Karachi, following their current online application and appointment process.
  5. Attend your appointment / interview, prepared to explain your study plan clearly and honestly.
  6. Travel and register: Anmeldung, then convert your visa to a residence permit in Germany.

Processing times vary by season, so apply as early as your admission allows. Our Visa & Beyond package includes a full file review and mock interviews.

🏅 Scholarships for Pakistani students

  • DAAD scholarships, including programmes for development-related postgraduate courses (EPOS) for professionals with work experience
  • Deutschlandstipendium: merit-based support awarded by universities to enrolled students
  • Political foundations (e.g. Heinrich Böll, Friedrich Ebert, Konrad Adenauer), each with its own focus and criteria
  • University-specific fee waivers and assistantships, especially for master's and PhD students

Deadlines are often a year in advance, so plan early. We include scholarship guidance in our Visa & Beyond and Concierge packages.

FAQ

Study in Germany from Pakistan: quick answers

Is IELTS required for a Germany student visa from Pakistan?

Language proof is required for your admission. For English-taught programmes most universities ask for IELTS, though some accept an MOI letter or other tests. Your visa file must show the language proof your university accepted. A strong IELTS score generally makes both steps smoother.

How many years of education do I need for a master's in Germany?

Usually 16 years. A 4-year BS/BE degree, or a 14-year BA/BSc plus a 2-year MA/MSc, typically qualifies. A 14-year degree alone is generally not enough for a German master's.

Can I take my family to Germany as a student?

Family reunification for students is possible in some cases but comes with extra financial and housing requirements. Most students go alone first and plan family reunification after finding work.

How long does the whole process take?

Plan for 6–9 months from your first profile check to arriving in Germany, and longer if you still need IELTS or German. Starting 10–12 months ahead of your target intake is ideal.

Can I stay in Germany after my studies?

Yes. Graduates can apply for an 18-month residence permit to look for work. Once employed in a qualified job, you can move to an EU Blue Card or skilled-worker permit, which can lead to permanent residence.
